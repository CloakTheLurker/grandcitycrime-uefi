heights per roof part and roof colours, same box as the map

laser scan: noaa's 2019 topobathy point cloud, from the usgs public lidar bucket on aws (usgs-lidar-public), public domain
python lidar/fetch.py lidar/raw 10 FL_TopobathyFLKeysNOAA_Hydroflattened_2019 -80.205 25.758 -80.115 25.800
    about 1,350 laz files, 394 MB, 48.8 million points
python lidar/dsm.py lidar/raw lidar
    needs laspy, lazrs and numpy. writes lidar/top.npy (highest point per 1.12 m cell) and lidar/ground.npy

aerial photos: usda naip from the usgs national map image service, public domain
python naip/fetch.py
    six tiles into naip/raw, stitched to naip/miami.png, 1e-5 degrees per pixel

python lidar/roofs.py
    needs scipy and pillow. splits every outline into its height levels, reads a roof colour, writes lidar/parts.json
ground from the photos: the same naip photos, the infrared band too (naip/fetch.py gets both)
python naip/cells.py
    averages photo colour, infrared and scan height over every 2.8 m ground cell, writes naip/cells.npz
parking areas: openstreetmap amenity=parking, the query is osm/parking.txt, saved as osm/parking.json
python osm/bake.py
    uses lidar/parts.json when it is there
    uses naip/cells.npz and osm/parking.json when they are there: green cells become grass, parking areas become lots, naip/ground.py does the sorting
    trees: green cells more than 4.5 m above the ground in the scan, one tree per crown, palms told from broad trees by how wide the green is round the top

the scan stops short of the west edge of the map (west of about -80.197) and the top 250 m, those buildings keep the county heights
